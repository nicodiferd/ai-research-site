🌌 Movement Isn't Distance, It's Frequency

Darryl Anka's explanation of how advanced beings don't travel through space — they change the variable of where they exist

What if the reason we haven't figured out interstellar travel isn't that the distances are too vast — but that we're thinking about movement wrong? A conversation between @antoinetteandfriends and a guest presents the Bashar framework: advanced civilizations don't cross space. They change the frequency variable that defines where an object exists.

The Core Inversion

The foundational idea is a conceptual flip that sounds simple but has radical implications:

"An object doesn't exist in a location. A location exists in an object."

Every object is made of energy. Among all the variables that define it — its mass, composition, temperature — one variable represents where it is in space and time. Its locational frequency. When an object moves from point A to point B, what actually changed is that variable's value.

We perceive movement as traversing the space between two points. The Bashar model proposes that sufficiently advanced technology can isolate the locational variable and change it directly — causing the object to stop existing at point A and instantly start existing at point B, without crossing the space between.

Two Tiers of Travel

Short Range
Electromagnetic / Gravitic Fields

Conventional physics, slower speeds, actual traversal of space

Interstellar
Locational Variable Shifting

Frequency manipulation, instantaneous, no space traversed

The distinction matters: this isn't a model where all travel works through frequency. Short-distance movement still uses physical fields. The frequency mechanism is specifically for the problem we've always considered impossible — leaping between star systems.

The Cross-Source Echo

The video's most interesting moment is the claimed convergence between two independent sources. Darryl Anka's Bashar has described this locational-variable mechanism for years. Then the creator read Custodians by Dolores Cannon — a completely different body of work based on hypnotic regression sessions — and found this passage:

"We just dial in a different locational variable and just pop there." — Custodians, p. 224

Same concept, same terminology, from sources that (the creator claims) developed independently. Whether this represents genuine convergence or coincidental phrasing is the question the video leaves open.

The Framework's Elegance and Its Limits

As a conceptual model, the locational-variable framework is elegant. It resolves the core problem of interstellar travel — the impossibility of crossing vast distances within a human lifespan — by redefining what movement is. It also maps loosely onto quantum mechanics concepts like quantum entanglement and the idea that particles don't have definite positions until observed.

But elegance isn't evidence. The framework is unfalsifiable in its current form: it makes no testable predictions, provides no mechanism for how locational variables are isolated or manipulated, and relies entirely on sources (channeled entities, hypnotic regression) that lack scientific validation.

Questions Worth Sitting With

  • How does frequency shifting avoid relativistic constraints?
  • Is the Bashar-Cannon convergence meaningful, or is "locational variable" a natural phrase anyone describing this concept would use?
  • Could this framework ever generate a testable hypothesis?

The value of content like this isn't in its truth claims — it's in the conceptual exercise. Inverting "objects move through space" into "space is a property of objects" is the kind of frame shift that, historically, has preceded genuine scientific breakthroughs. Whether this particular inversion leads anywhere is another question entirely.
