The Evolution: From LPR to Full Surveillance Grid

Founded

Flock Safety launches as a license plate reader (LPR) company, marketing to neighborhood HOAs and small police departments as a tool for solving vehicle theft.

2018

Patent filed for a "dynamic surveillance network" — the foundational architecture that would eventually connect individual cameras into a unified national intelligence platform.

Early 2025

All existing LPR cameras are upgraded — at no additional cost to operators — to full video cameras with AI-powered person and vehicle detection. What were single-purpose plate readers become general-purpose surveillance nodes.

The Product Stack

Flock Safety has expanded from a single camera product into a suite of interconnected surveillance tools:

Core Cameras

Full video + AI. Detect vehicles and people. Store minimum 30 days, up to 5 years if law enforcement subscribes to extended retention.

Wing Gateway

Converts ANY existing IP camera into a Flock node. Gas station cameras, ATMs, Ring doorbells, commercial CCTV — all can feed the network.

Raven

Audio sensor module. Currently detects gunshots for rapid law enforcement response. Researcher notes likely expansion to general audio monitoring.

FlockNova

The intelligence platform. Takes a single identifier and expands it to hundreds of data points. Natural language query interface across the full network.

FlockNova: The Identity Expansion Engine

The most significant capability described by the researcher is FlockNova — Flock Safety's intelligence platform that functions as a data expansion engine. The input can be virtually any identifier associated with a person:

Starting Identifiers FlockNova Can Use

  • License plate number
  • Full name
  • Email address
  • Phone number
  • Social Security Number
  • IP address
  • Cryptocurrency wallet address
  • Credit or debit card number

From any one of these, the researcher claims FlockNova can expand to hundreds of associated data points — cross-referencing camera footage, location history, vehicle registrations, and connected data sources to build a comprehensive picture of an individual's movements and associations.

"When Flock Safety says their cameras don't capture personal information — that's technically true. But a license plate is bound to an identity. And an identity, in their system, is the key that unlocks everything else."

Geofencing and Natural Language Queries

Two capabilities stand out as particularly significant for their scope:

Geofencing: Law enforcement can draw a virtual boundary around any address or location and request a list of all license plates (and therefore all identities) that were detected within that zone during a specified time period. In practice: identify everyone who attended a meeting, visited a home, or was present at any location.

Natural Language Queries: The system supports queries like "find a man with a green hat and a blue shirt." This query — entered in plain English — searches the entire national camera network for visual matches. No plate number needed. Physical description alone can trigger a nationwide sweep.

The Warrant Question

The researcher emphasizes what may be the most consequential aspect of the system: its relationship to legal process.

  • In most jurisdictions, accessing Flock Safety data requires no warrant
  • Some jurisdictions require only a case number — not judicial approval
  • The data lives on private company servers, not government systems, affecting what Fourth Amendment protections apply
  • The "third party doctrine" in US law holds that data voluntarily shared with a third party has reduced constitutional protection

A Real Case: The Fugitive Chain

📋 How the System Works in Practice

The researcher describes a documented case that illustrates how the data layers compound:

  1. Fugitive's license plate flagged in the Flock network
  2. FlockNova used to identify the fugitive's home address from plate + identity data
  3. Fugitive not found at home — but food delivery app records (obtained separately) reveal a frequent delivery address for a known associate
  4. Fugitive arrested at associate's address

Each step uses a different data source. The Flock system provided the initial identity chain; commercial data sources completed the picture. No warrant was required for the Flock data at any step.

The Technical Distinction That Does Heavy Lifting

The researcher's core critique of Flock Safety's public communications comes down to a technical distinction being used to obscure a practical reality:

"Flock Safety's statement that their cameras 'don't capture personal information' is technically accurate in a very narrow sense. A camera sees a license plate. A license plate is a number. Numbers aren't personal information. But every plate is registered to a vehicle, every vehicle is registered to a person, and that person is linked to their entire digital life. The technical denial is true. The practical privacy protection is zero."

The question raised is not whether Flock Safety is doing anything illegal — by most accounts, its operations are within current law. The question is whether current law is adequate to the capabilities now being deployed, and whether the public understands that the country's existing camera infrastructure has been quietly transformed into a unified AI-powered surveillance network.
