🕊️ Grief Is Love Expanding

A meditation where a creator witnessed her own funeral — and discovered what it means to fully inhabit being human

TikTok @karinhadadan · 197,400 views · 22,800 likes · Feb 4, 2026

Karin has been asking the same question since childhood: Why are we here? Not as a philosophical exercise, but as a felt urgency — the sense that existing without understanding the point of existence is its own particular suffering. She shares, in this video with nearly 200,000 views, what a meditation gave her that years of searching hadn't: not an answer exactly, but something better — a direct experience.

The Meditation: Witnessing Your Own Funeral

What She Saw

Karin entered a meditation that shifted into an unexpected vision. She found herself watching her own funeral from outside her body — a third-person perspective, observing.

She saw the people she loves, gathered and grieving. She saw a memorial sign bearing her name and the dates of her life: 1996–2025. She saw her own body in an open casket.

And then — something she did not expect — she felt the most profound peace she had ever experienced in her life.

"Not peace like relief. Peace like recognition. Like: this is exactly right. This is complete."

Three Things She Knew

In the meditation state, Karin describes knowing three things with an immediacy that required no reasoning:

  • 1
    She lived well and loved well.
    Not perfectly. Not without mistakes. But the orientation of her life had been toward connection and love, and that was enough. More than enough. It was, in the vision, the whole measure.
  • 2
    Her soul is eternal; her body is temporary.
    The body in the casket was a vehicle. The thing that was watching the funeral — her consciousness, her awareness, whatever we call the witness — that was still present and still experiencing. Death was the end of the vehicle, not the end of the driver.
  • 3
    It was her time because she had fulfilled her purpose.
    Not because she was old, or sick, or because the universe was indifferent. Because what she came to do, she did. There was a kind of completion in it that felt like permission rather than loss.

The Scene Shifts: Her Parents' Funeral

Then the meditation moved to somewhere harder. The scene shifted, and now Karin was watching her parents' funeral. She saw their names on a memorial sign. She saw the milestones that lay ahead — her own engagement, her wedding — with the felt understanding that they would not be there. Not physically.

This is where the peace broke, and the grief arrived.

"I tried to find something positive to focus on. I couldn't. The pain was there and the positive thought wasn't strong enough to replace it. And then something happened that I hadn't planned: I surrendered."

She stopped trying to manage the grief. She let it be what it was. And what happened next is the heart of what she's sharing.

What Surrender Revealed

When she stopped trying to transmute the grief into something more tolerable, the grief itself changed. It didn't diminish — it opened.

What she found inside the grief was not less love, but exponentially more of it. The pain of imagining her wedding without her parents did not make that milestone feel diminished — it revealed the depth of love that was already present in it. Her parents, she understood in the vision, would not be absent from those moments. They would be in them — woven into the joy, which would carry their presence as an essential thread.

"Grief is simply love expanding its capacity to receive more love." — Karin Hadadan

This reframe is not consolation. It's a structural claim about what grief actually is: not love minus the person, but love encountering the full weight of its own depth. The grief is proportional to the love. Which means grief, rather than being love's enemy, is love's measure — and its teacher.

After the Meditation

Karin describes coming out of the meditation changed in a specific, practical way:

  • She no longer experiences her parents as separate from her
  • She finds herself less focused on their individual flaws or triggering behaviors
  • When she saw her parents after the meditation, her immediate desire was simply to embrace them
  • The abstract question "why are we here?" has become less urgent — replaced by a felt sense rather than an intellectual answer

The Teaching She Offers

"We are not here to transcend human experience. We are here to fully inhabit it. The grief, the joy, the loss, the love — not as obstacles to the spiritual life, but as the spiritual life itself. The body is not the cage. It's the instrument."

She closes with a line that has clearly resonated with her audience: "The answers are within you. This memory is within you." The implication is that the peace she found in the meditation wasn't imported from outside — it was something she accessed that was already there, waiting for the right conditions to be felt.

On Being Human

The video returns, at the end, to the question Karin has been asking since childhood. And her answer — insofar as she has one now — is not philosophical or theological. It is experiential:

"We are meant to remember our divine essence and live from that place. Not to escape being human — to be more fully human than we usually allow ourselves to be. The love we feel for each other is the divine essence expressing itself through us. We are not separate from it. We are it."

What makes this video unusual in the self-development space is its willingness to sit with grief rather than immediately resolve it. The movement is not from pain to peace through avoidance, but through grief to a deeper peace than was available without the grief. The surrender is not defeat — it is the thing that opens the door.
