🌌 Bashar's Five Laws of Existence

A channeled entity's framework for the structure of reality — from existence itself to the law of attraction reframed

For over four decades, a being called Bashar — claimed to be an extraterrestrial consciousness from the civilization Essassani, channeled through the medium Darryl Anka — has been presenting what it describes as the fundamental laws governing the structure of existence. A video from @positivevibrations.only, with 375,000 views, distills these teachings into five laws that have resonated widely across spiritual and self-development communities.

Whatever one's position on the channeling phenomenon itself, the framework Bashar offers is internally consistent and contains insights that parallel both ancient philosophical traditions and aspects of modern physics. Here are the five laws as Bashar presents them.

The Five Laws

LAW 1

You Exist

"You cannot not-exist."

The first law is also the most fundamental: existence itself cannot be negated. You cannot step outside of existence to verify it — the very act of questioning requires existence. Bashar frames this as the foundational certainty: you are.

This echoes Descartes' cogito and the Vedantic concept of Sat (pure being) — the indestructible substrate beneath all experience. Even death, in this framework, is a change of state rather than cessation. Existence is not a property you can lose.

Implication: No permanent annihilation is possible. Fear of non-existence is based on a logical impossibility.

LAW 2

Everything Is Here and Now

"Time and space are illusions. All moments exist simultaneously."

What we experience as linear time is, in Bashar's framework, a perceptual artifact. All moments — past, present, and future — exist simultaneously. What separates them is not duration but frequency.

The television channel analogy is central here: a TV set doesn't create the programs it displays. They all exist simultaneously in the electromagnetic spectrum. The tuner selects which frequency to express. Similarly, "moving through time" is not forward motion through a sequence — it is the consciousness shifting between frequencies of simultaneous, already-existing moments.

The implication: You cannot change the past because the "past" moment still exists, unchanged, at its frequency. What you can change is which version of "now" you tune to. This reframes healing from "fixing what happened" to "shifting which moment you are currently experiencing as primary reality."

LAW 3

The One Is the All; the All Is the One

"There is only one thing. Everything is made from it."

This law states that all of existence is a single unified substance expressing itself in infinite variety. Separation is a structural feature of experience — like waves on an ocean — not a fundamental metaphysical reality.

This parallels: the Vedantic concept of Brahman, the Hermetic "All is One," Taoist cosmology (the Tao as undifferentiated ground), and aspects of modern physics (the quantum field as the foundational substrate from which all particles emerge as excitations).

The practical meaning: your experience of isolation or separateness is a local viewing condition, not the ultimate architecture of things.

LAW 4

What You Put Out Is What You Get Back

"It's not about learning to attract. It's about removing what blocks what you already attract."

This is Law 4 — and where Bashar most directly challenges mainstream Law of Attraction teaching. The conventional understanding: think positive thoughts to attract positive things. Bashar's version is structurally different.

The Critical Reframe: Law of Attraction Inverted

The most practically significant element of the five laws is how Bashar repositions the fourth. The conventional Law of Attraction framing asks: "How do I attract what I want?" Bashar's answer: you're asking the wrong question.

Conventional Teaching

You are currently not vibrating at the frequency of what you want. You need to learn a new vibration and then send it out to attract what you desire.

The work is about addition — acquiring new states.

Bashar's Version

You already are the vibration of what you desire — it's your natural frequency. The problem is that beliefs are acting as resistance, blocking what you naturally attract.

The work is about removal — clearing what's in the way.

"It is not a problem of needing to learn how to attract. It is a problem of needing to learn how to stop blocking what you naturally attract. You are a magnet. The question is: what are you covering the magnet with?"

This reframe has significant implications for spiritual practice. If you're working on "raising your vibration," the question becomes: are you trying to acquire something you don't have, or release something that's covering what you do have? The latter approach tends to look less like affirmation and more like emotional processing, belief-system examination, and the removal of learned limitations.

LAW 5

Everything Changes Except the First Four Laws

"Change is the only constant."

The fifth law completes the framework: everything in manifest existence is in constant flux. Forms change, relationships change, beliefs change, even civilizations change. The only stable reference points are the four laws themselves, which operate as the unchanging structure within which all change occurs.

This parallels Heraclitus ("you cannot step into the same river twice"), Buddhist impermanence (anicca), and the thermodynamic principle that all systems tend toward change over time. For Bashar, the practical teaching is: resistance to change is the source of suffering; alignment with change is the source of flow.

The Framework as a Whole

Taken together, the five laws form a nested architecture:

  • Law 1 (You Exist) — establishes the indestructible ground
  • Law 2 (Here and Now) — locates you in the only moment where change is possible
  • Law 3 (One Is All) — removes the illusion of separation that makes attraction seem "difficult"
  • Law 4 (Removal not Addition) — gives you the practical method
  • Law 5 (Change) — contextualizes the whole as dynamic rather than static

Whether approached as channeled transmission, philosophical framework, or psychological heuristic, the Bashar five laws continue to resonate because they address a genuine problem: the gap between what we want and what we experience, and the confusion about why conventional self-improvement efforts often don't close that gap.

"You are not trying to become something you are not. You are trying to remember what you always have been — and stop pretending otherwise."
